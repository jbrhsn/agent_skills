# X mechanics and evidence boundaries

Research checked against official sources on 2026-09-19. These are dated observations, not permanent guarantees. Recheck relevant official documentation when account eligibility, a near-limit draft, or a changing feature affects delivery. Ordinary copy editing does not require researching every platform feature.

## Post types and capability

X documents a standard 280-character post and longer single posts up to 25,000 characters. Longer posts require a Premium subscription. Source: [types of posts](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/types-of-posts).

The [Premium guide](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-premium) lists longer posts among features available across its subscription tiers, and cautions that availability can vary by platform and location. Do not infer the author's account eligibility from their request or a verification badge. A longer single post and an X Article are distinct formats; this skill's long mode means a longer post.

Documentation can disagree: the [posting guide](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post) contains platform-specific instructions mentioning 4,000 as well as 25,000 characters. Prefer current evidence for the target account/client when it matters. State unresolved capability assumptions; do not manufacture certainty from conflicting pages or make a purchase recommendation just to finish a draft.

X describes a [thread](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/create-a-thread) as connected posts by one person. Budget each post independently. Use standard-length posts by default for portability; use longer units only when requested or appropriate for confirmed capability. Thread structure is an editorial choice, not evidence of better distribution.

## Count the text that will be published

The official [counting guide](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters) describes weighted length: ordinary Latin characters generally count as one; CJK and emoji as two. Recognized URLs count as 23 regardless of visible length. Emoji sequences and Unicode normalization complicate naive counting. Use X's composer or an available compatible `twitter-text` parser for exact standard-post checks; ordinary string length is not equivalent. Count links, whitespace, hashtags, and in-copy numbering. Exclude delivery labels and notes.

If no compatible validator is available, identify the method as approximate or unchecked and leave headroom. Do not claim an exact X count or verified fit. For longer posts, verify the target composer's rules separately; a standard-post parser's validity flag cannot establish longer-post eligibility.

Correct an over-limit draft without discarding essential qualifications. If exact validation requires unavailable tooling or account access, deliver the useful draft with that specific remaining check; do not require a login just to write.

## Formatting, media, and distribution

Use readable plain text unless the requested client and feature support a richer format. Markdown link syntax and document headings in a delivery file are not evidence of native formatting support.

X supports image descriptions; consult its [image-description guidance](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/add-image-descriptions) when packaging actual media. Keep important evidence available in text rather than assuming every reader can interpret an image.

Separate verified product constraints from editorial preferences and performance hypotheses. Do not prescribe universal best posting times, optimal thread lengths, hashtag counts, link penalties, or guarantees of reach. When the user asks about performance, identify the source, date, audience, and measurement limitations; supplied analytics can suggest experiments but do not prove causation.
