# Closing and calls to action

End in a way that fulfills the article's promise. A synthesis, practical decision rule, next step, narrative resolution, or unresolved question can work. A summary is useful when it helps consolidate a complex explanation; avoid mechanically restating every section.

Do not force an ending length or a three-part formula. Tutorials may finish with verification and limits; case studies with what transfers; arguments with a consequence; experience reports with a changed understanding.

A CTA is optional. Use a genuine question when the writer wants discussion, an action when the reader can reasonably take it, or an actual supplied destination when relevant to the user's purpose. Do not invent newsletters, products, publication schedules, or links. Promotional material should be transparent and proportionate.

Do not manufacture a question the author claims not to know merely to stimulate engagement. For consequential health, finance, or legal subjects, keep the conclusion within the evidence and distinguish general information from individualized guidance.

Record CTA choice in publishing notes only when helpful; “none” is a valid result. The strongest final line may need nothing after it.
