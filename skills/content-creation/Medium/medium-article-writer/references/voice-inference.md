# Voice inference

Read the author's material and instructions before choosing register. Separate their prose from quotations, pasted research, and dictated false starts. Do not infer a biography or expertise level from the topic.

Identify useful signals: formality, audience knowledge, terminology, rhythm, humor, degree of certainty, and first-person attribution. Preserve strong expressions where they remain clear and accurate. A brief voice note is useful for long pieces, but fixed line counts and verbatim quotas are unnecessary.

Thin notes can support a clear neutral draft. Ask for a writing sample only when voice fidelity materially matters and available evidence is insufficient. Never fill that gap with invented experiences or mannerisms.

During revision, compare the opening, middle, and ending for consistency. Check that uncertainty stayed intact, I/we ownership is accurate, and sourced facts remain distinct from the author's experience. Improvements to grammar or structure need not preserve every awkward phrase.

Describe significant omissions or editorial reframing so the user can revise cheaply. A request to condense authorizes ordinary cuts; it does not authorize changing the conclusion or deleting explicitly requested evidence.
