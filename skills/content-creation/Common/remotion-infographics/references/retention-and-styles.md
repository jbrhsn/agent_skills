# Retention research and style choices

## Evidence and limits

Research reviewed on 2026-09-20. Refresh platform trends during each project. These sources support design decisions, not a guarantee that any one style will outperform another.

| Source | Useful guidance | Limit and application |
|---|---|---|
| [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads) | Early hooks, a hook/body/close structure, text overlays, purposeful editing, sound, and interface-safe framing. | Advertiser guidance drawing on studies largely from 2020–2022. Ad recall and brand outcomes are not organic infographic retention. Use as directional guidance, not a quantified performance promise. |
| [YouTube Shorts creator discussion, January 2025](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) | Immediate audience interest and compact stories suited to short video. | A creator's first-second advice is practical experience, not a universal experimentally established threshold. |
| [TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/creative/creativeCenter) | A place to inspect current trends and ad examples by available market and category filters. | Record the date, filters, example URLs, and visible metrics. Featured examples and paid distribution are a selected sample; do not equate popularity or CTR with retention. |
| [YouTube audience retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en) | Examine where viewers leave, continue, or rewatch to guide revisions. | Reporting availability varies. A spike can reflect interest or confusion; inspect the scene before interpreting it. |

The pacing ranges and attention mechanisms below are design hypotheses to test for the specific audience. Fast pacing means useful progress without dead time; text overload and constant motion can make an explanation harder to follow. Do not describe a design as scientifically proven without research supporting that exact claim.

## Six adaptable style variants

These are a reusable style vocabulary, not a list of currently trending or proven highest-retention formats. Connect the chosen treatment to freshly observed platform patterns when evidence supports it. Palette examples are starting points; check actual text/background contrast and honor the author's brand.

| Style | Best fit and hook | Visual grammar and pacing | Attention mechanism and tradeoff | Example palette: background / text / accent | Final hero |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kinetic editorial | A strong thesis or misconception; open with the article's most useful concise contrast. | Large typography, one highlighted phrase, quick text replacements with a stable diagram anchor. | Salience and expectation/resolution; too much word-by-word animation slows reading and can distort the author's emphasis. | `#101010` / `#FAFAFA` / `#FACC15` | Complete takeaway beside one explanatory visual. |
| Data spotlight | One verified statistic or comparison; introduce the question the data answers. | Reveal labels before values, build bars or dots, then highlight the relevant comparison. | Concrete evidence and visual contrast; retain units, denominator, date, and uncertainty. Avoid counting theatrics that imply false observations. | `#0B1220` / `#F8FAFC` / `#38BDF8` | Labeled chart, key conclusion, and compact source credit. |
| Before/after split | A source-supported change, tradeoff, or alternative approach; show the contrast immediately. | Aligned split panels, matched objects, then transform only the differing factors. | Comparison reduces the work needed to spot differences; avoid fake before/after evidence or implying causation from a mere contrast. | `#F8FAFC` / `#0F172A` / `#0F766E` | Both states with one clear annotation of what changed. |
| Blueprint flow | Systems, workflows, and technical explainers; reveal the bottleneck or outcome first. | Precise lines, restrained grid, sequential nodes and connectors, one active route at a time. | Progressive disclosure and cause/effect tracing; avoid tiny labels and too many branches. | `#081827` / `#E6F4FF` / `#67E8F9` | Complete simplified flow with the important path highlighted. |
| Paper cutout explainer | Personal insight, conceptual advice, or a relatable problem; start with a concrete recognizable situation. | Flat paper shapes, restrained texture, annotations and purposeful snap transitions. | Familiar objects and a small narrative can make abstract ideas approachable; excessive stickers or jitter compete with meaning. | `#FFF7ED` / `#292524` / `#C2410C` | One coherent annotated model with a practical takeaway. |
| Modular card stack | A short framework or two to three actionable steps; lead with the benefit or first useful step. | Cards reveal in sequence, carry visible progress, then settle into an overview. | Chunking and visible progress orient the viewer; avoid listicle padding or revealing everything at once. | `#F5F3FF` / `#1E1B4B` / `#6D28D9` | All essential steps in a balanced readable layout. |

Use color to establish hierarchy and reinforce consistent categories. Support distinctions with labels, shapes, or position as well as hue. Do not infer emotions, credibility, or audience identity from a palette alone.

## Storyboard and pacing

For each beat record its time range, on-screen copy, visual action, informational value, and evidence or qualification. A possible 20-second sequence is 0–2 seconds for a truthful hook, 2–6 for context or the first result, 6–13 for the core mechanism, 13–17 for its practical implication, and 17–20 for a settled hero. This is an adjustable example, not a template to impose on every article.

For 10 seconds, reduce the scope to a single contrast or transformation; for 30 seconds, spend the extra time on explanation or an example. Do not simply speed up a longer script. Each change should reveal information, guide attention, or resolve a question. Read the copy at realistic viewing size and watch the complete sequence; allow more time for unfamiliar terminology, numbers, or labels.

Keep the payoff faithful to the source. Avoid fake urgency, fabricated controversy, exaggerated certainty, and unrelated attention bait. A viewer should leave with a useful understanding even without clicking through to the article.

## Learning from actual results

When the user provides analytics, compare clips of similar duration, platform, audience, and distribution conditions. Look at initial hold or viewed-versus-swiped metrics where available, average view duration, percentage viewed, completion, rewatches, and useful engagement such as saves. Do not prescribe a universal success threshold or infer unavailable metrics from view counts.

Form one hypothesis from an observed problem, such as an unclear opening or a dense chart at a dropout point. Change one major variable for a subsequent version when practical. Document confounders and distinguish comprehension problems from weak relevance. Never claim a controlled A/B result from unrelated organic posts.
