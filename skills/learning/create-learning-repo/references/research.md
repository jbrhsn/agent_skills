# Research for a learning plan

Research when it resolves a meaningful uncertainty, verifies current expectations, or is requested. A complete supplied plan may still need a fact check; a stable introductory roadmap may need no live research. Follow the environment's research requirements.

Useful sources depend on the goal:

- Official documentation, standards, and changelogs for technical details.
- Official syllabi and released assessments for exams.
- Relevant role descriptions and first-hand accounts for interview preparation.
- Published artifacts and practitioner analysis for craft.
- Appropriate research and clearly identified observations for practice-oriented learning.

Prefer primary sources for factual claims. Practitioner reports can inform context but do not establish universal requirements. When sources disagree, weigh relevance and credibility; do not automatically add a chapter for every disputed topic.

Turn research into better decisions: what to include, omit, reorder, or teach more deeply, and why. Useful depth notes describe a capability, such as “compare two designs under the stated constraints,” rather than another list of nouns.

Record consulted sources where they help readers trace the plan's decisions, usually in plan research notes. A source may also belong in a chapter brief when it is part of the assignment. Do not invent citations or imply that the learner has read them. If verification is unavailable, identify consequential uncertainty and continue with supported material.
