# Opening options

An opening earns attention by giving the intended reader a reason to continue. It may state the answer directly; predictability is not failure when readers need a clear announcement, result, or explanation.

Choose from the actual material:

- A concrete result, with its conditions and evidence.
- A real moment or decision that makes the stakes clear.
- A useful question the body answers.
- A defensible claim followed by evidence and limits.
- A direct announcement explaining who benefits and how.
- A contrast that illuminates a real tradeoff rather than manufacturing controversy.

Generate alternatives when the angle is uncertain or the user requests options; no fixed number is required. Keep the most useful promise near the beginning. Feed truncation varies by device, format, and interface; verify preview behavior instead of treating a character threshold as universal.

If the next paragraph adds needed context, use it. A mandatory “re-hook” often repeats the opening. Check that title/opening, body, and close form one argument.

Reject fabricated numbers, inflated stakes, unsupported “nobody talks about” claims, and withheld answers that the body never delivers. Preserve necessary qualifications even when a shorter hook sounds punchier.
