# Visual directions

Start with the user's brief or existing brand/house style. Otherwise propose a coherent direction suited to the article. White backgrounds, limited palettes, and simple forms are useful options, not universal requirements.

| Article need | Direction to consider | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct items or tools | Editorial object series, consistent framing | Repetitive generic icons |
| Process or tutorial | Mechanism illustration or structured flow diagram | Art mistaken for an accurate technical specification |
| Case study | Before/after, tension/resolution, or actual evidence | Invented screenshots or exaggerated failure |
| Argument | One clear metaphor or contrast | Metaphors implying stronger claims than the text |
| Explainer | Layers, relationships, scale, annotated diagram | Missing labels or misleading proportions |
| Experience | Meaningful setting or object, supplied photo, editorial scene | Fictional scene mistaken for the author's actual event |

Choose palette, line/detail level, rendering, and recurring motifs that support coherence. Images may vary in perspective and ratio to fit their content. State which properties should stay consistent, rather than requiring every prompt to repeat an entire block verbatim.

Use precise colors or supplied references when matching matters. Do not assert a color has a universal emotional meaning. For accurate brand assets or diagrams, specify the necessary source/layout approach instead of asking a model to approximate critical details.
