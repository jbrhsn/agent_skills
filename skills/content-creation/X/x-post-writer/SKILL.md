# X Post Writer

Deliver useful X writing in the author's voice, choosing depth and structure to fit the requested outcome. Single posts and threads share source, voice, and evidence checks; their composition differs. This skill works without a strategy document, other skills, analytics, or account access.

## Establish the assignment

Read the supplied text, named source, and relevant existing draft. Accept a topic, notes, research, article, or another platform's post; `source.md` is a convention, not a prerequisite. Identify the reader, purpose, central point, supporting material, language, voice, requested format, and any length constraint. Preserve original notes and unrelated edits.

Use explicit preferences and the author's own prose as voice evidence. Distinguish quotations and copied research from the author's writing. Preserve I/we attribution, uncertainty, technical detail that matters to the reader, and the author's position. With sparse input, use a clear, direct voice rather than inventing personality or experience.

Proceed with ordinary editorial choices. Ask only when missing information materially affects meaning or feasibility, such as an essential personal result, contradictory required claims, or incompatible format and account constraints. A topic alone can support an explanatory or opinion draft without fabricating a first-person story. Do not impose a hook-selection or outline-approval gate.

## Choose the format

Honor an explicit format. Otherwise choose the smallest form that delivers the intended substance. Read [composition guidance](references/composition.md) for format selection and substantial drafting or conversion.

- Short post: one concise observation, announcement, question, or takeaway.
- Medium-length post: one developed point, usually with context, an example, or a qualification.
- Long post: a sustained explanation, story, or argument that benefits from continuous reading.
- Thread: connected posts whose sequence adds value through steps, examples, narrative progression, or distinct arguments.

These are editorial descriptions, not fixed character bands. Short and medium drafts can cross a platform limit; long posts are not automatically threads. Do not produce all formats unless requested. For an unspecified format and unknown account capabilities, prefer a standard-length single post when it can carry the substance, or a thread of standard-length posts when sequencing helps.

Read [X mechanics](references/x-mechanics.md) when establishing limits or account requirements, checking final fit, or advising on platform behavior. If a requested longer single post requires unconfirmed account capability, draft it and flag that requirement outside the copy. If the user explicitly lacks that capability, explain the conflict and offer compression or a thread; do not silently change an explicitly requested format.

## Write and substantiate

Make the opening's promise specific and fulfill it. Preserve useful nuance during compression. For a thread, write connected units rather than cutting prose at the character boundary; keep essential qualifications with the claims they limit. Follow the selected format guidance without mandatory hook formulas, thread counts, hashtag quotas, or calls to action.

Verify changing or consequential factual claims against appropriate primary sources. Research should support the requested piece; an evergreen edit with sufficient supplied evidence need not become a trend-research project. Never invent personal experience, metrics, quotations, credentials, results, or citations. Attribute outside evidence and label illustrative examples. When adapting a LinkedIn post or Medium article, rebuild the opening and structure for X while preserving its factual and editorial meaning.

If an essential claim cannot be verified, make its unresolved status visible and avoid presenting the draft as publication-ready. Qualify or omit unsupported peripheral claims transparently; do not bury missing support in notes while leaving an unequivocal claim in the copy. Adding “possibly” does not justify a numerical estimate without a stated basis: preserve a supported qualitative observation and explain the omitted figure. If the user explicitly requires that number, request its basis or clearly frame it as an untested hypothesis or target only when that matches the source. Keep source attribution readable inside the post where needed, with fuller provenance in notes. Use actual URLs in paste-ready text, not Markdown link syntax.

## Use optional context

Read [handoff conventions](references/handoffs.md) when strategy, research, another writer's artifact, or a multi-piece workflow is in scope. Relevant supplied or clearly associated `x_strategy.md`, `kresearch.md`, and previous drafts can improve the piece but never become mandatory prerequisites. Follow the current request when it overrides older context. Use other skills only if their work is needed and available; do not automatically launch research, strategy creation, or a whole content pipeline.

## Check and deliver

Compare the final copy with the source for meaning, attribution, omissions, and support. Check voice, opening payoff, accessibility, and format fit. For threads, inspect both the sequence and each post in isolation; measure every post after final edits, including any published numbering and links. Follow the counting method and fallback in [X mechanics](references/x-mechanics.md); never label a guessed or ordinary string count as a verified X count.

For a folder workflow, save a single post as `x_post.md` or a thread as `x_thread.md`. Single-post files contain only the copy. In thread files use `## Post 1`, `## Post 2`, and so on as delivery labels, with paste-ready text under each; those labels are not published text. Optional in-copy numbering must be counted. Use distinct filenames for requested variants and preserve existing user work. Explicit paths and inline-only requests take precedence.

Create `x_post_notes.md` or `x_thread_notes.md` only when useful, adapting [the notes template](assets/writing-notes-template.md). Keep counts, sources, capability assumptions, material editorial choices, and unresolved checks outside the copy. Note whether media/alt text is provisional or based on an inspected asset. Report paths or deliver the requested inline text, with any material readiness limitation. Drafting does not authorize publishing, scheduling, replying, or account changes.
