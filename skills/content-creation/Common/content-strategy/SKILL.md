# Content Strategy

Turn an audience need and an author's credible contribution into an achievable publishing plan. Idea discovery, research, and prioritization are included; no other skill, analytics export, account access, or strategy file is required. Support a single platform or a coordinated plan, and adapt the same method to other platforms after checking relevant constraints.

## Establish direction

Use the request and relevant supplied material to identify audience, purpose, niche, language, author perspective, platforms, planning period, capacity, and existing commitments. Accept notes, articles, previous plans, analytics, or a topic directly. Ask only when missing information materially changes the plan; otherwise state consequential assumptions and proceed. Do not invent the author's expertise, experiences, opinions, or results.

Resolve relative periods to explicit dates and distinguish a calendar month from four weeks. Use the user's time zone for dated schedules; if unknown, label the assumption or leave exact times unset. Infer a modest provisional cadence when none is specified and show its workload assumptions. Honor requested volume; surface conflicts with stated capacity and propose feasible tradeoffs rather than silently dropping commitments.

For an ideas-only request, deliver researched opportunities without forcing a calendar. For a revision, preserve completed items, user edits, stable item identifiers, and fixed dates unless the request changes them. Current instructions take precedence over older plans.

## Research and select

Read [research and prioritization](references/research-and-prioritization.md) when discovering or evaluating ideas. Research changing claims, timely topics, and explicit research requests with live sources when available. Evergreen or experience-led proposals can use supplied material; label them as editorial proposals when demand is unmeasured. Report unavailable research rather than presenting brainstorming as observed trends.

Build useful angles from reader problems and available evidence. Inspect promising sources beyond headlines, distinguish community interest from factual support, and record publication/event dates and retrieval dates where relevant. Prioritize by audience fit, author contribution, evidence, timing, effort, and the plan's objective. Explain the ordering without invented numerical precision or reach predictions. Include source gaps and author input needed before drafting.

Derive a manageable set of editorial pillars when useful. Each should express a reader problem and the value the author can consistently provide; no fixed pillar count, content ratio, or mandatory marketing funnel applies. Existing idea-research output can supplement this work, but invoking that skill or its helpers is optional.

## Build the plan

Read [calendar conventions](references/calendar-schema.md) for weekly/monthly calendars, workload checks, dependencies, and revisions. Read the relevant sections of [platform planning](references/platform-planning.md) when choosing formats or discussing distribution and metrics.

Choose formats and sequence to serve the material. Account for research, writing, media, review, and adaptation effort across all selected platforms. A cross-platform adaptation consumes capacity even when the research is shared. Make prerequisites and preparation deadlines visible; planned publication dates must follow required preparation. Reserve flexible slots only when useful, with a usable evergreen fallback and a research checkpoint for timely topics.

Each planned piece needs enough context to draft independently: reader, purpose, angle, format, core evidence or source status, and required original material. Connect adaptations with a shared source identifier, explaining the different reader promise or treatment on each platform. Do not fill the calendar by copying the same text everywhere or inventing future events. Dates are editorial targets, not scheduled account actions.

## Review and hand off

Check actual date/day agreement, requested period coverage, volume, workload, repeated angles, source readiness, and dependency order. Mark items needing evidence or author input as conditional, with an alternative when that gap threatens the plan. Do not call them ready to draft when their central claim is unsupported.

Define a small measurement plan aligned with the objective and metrics actually available. Without history, use the first period to establish a baseline; targets are aspirations, not forecasts. Compare similar formats and observation windows, keep platforms' metric definitions separate, and treat small samples as directional. Explain what evidence would lead to continuing, changing, or dropping a theme.

For saved plans, use `content_strategy.md` by convention and adapt [the template](assets/content-strategy-template.md). Use `content_research.md` only when evidence would overwhelm the main plan. Explicit filenames, dated folders, and inline-only requests take precedence; preserve existing unrelated work. Short plans need no extra files or empty template sections.

Read [handoffs](references/handoffs.md) when supplying writer briefs or using other skills' artifacts. Deliver the plan, material assumptions, evidence limitations, and next preparation steps. This skill prepares strategy and briefs; drafting complete pieces or taking publishing, scheduling, messaging, or account actions requires those actions to be in the user's request.
