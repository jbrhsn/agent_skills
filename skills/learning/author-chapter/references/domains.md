# Domain guidance

Choose evidence and examples that fit the field and requested outcome. These profiles are starting points; mixed or custom subjects can combine approaches without fitting a single template.

| Profile | Useful evidence and examples | Deeper understanding can include |
|---|---|---|
| `technical` | Specifications, reproducible derivations, runnable examples, measured behavior, documented incidents | Trade-offs, boundary conditions, operational consequences, alternatives |
| `craft` | Attributed excerpts, original before/after revisions, annotated artifacts | Audience effects, deliberate choices, competing interpretations |
| `practice` | Observed logs, research with stated limitations, clearly hypothetical routines | Adaptation to constraints, recovery after disruption, evaluation of outcomes |
| `exam` | Official syllabi, released questions, verified marking guidance, labelled original practice questions | Applying concepts under the actual assessment constraints |

Real examples and labelled constructed examples can both teach well. Attribute borrowed material and quote only as appropriate; summaries and original examples are valid when reproducing a source would be unnecessary or unsuitable.

For technical examples, distinguish calculations from benchmarks, include the context needed to interpret results, and state whether code was run. Avoid presenting a tutorial's happy path as sufficient evidence for a broader operational claim.

For craft, connect specific choices to plausible effects rather than relying on praise. Distinguish interpretation and taste from established fact; a useful technique is not a universal rule.

For practice, separate anecdote from research and hypothetical cases from actual logs. Discuss limitations that affect decisions and avoid guaranteed outcomes. Follow applicable verification requirements for health, legal, or financial material.

For exams, verify official format and syllabus claims or identify the uncertainty. Label additional enrichment when the distinction matters; an original practice question need not be mistaken for an official question.

For other domains, identify what would count as a convincing explanation or demonstration for the learner's task, then choose examples and review criteria accordingly.
