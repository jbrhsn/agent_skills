# Editorial review

Use the checks relevant to the request. Correct weaknesses in the draft rather than handing back a checklist of fixable issues.

- Does the title accurately promise what the article delivers?
- Can the intended reader follow the mechanism or argument, with needed context and useful examples?
- Are key claims supported by the cited sources or clearly attributed first-hand evidence? Check denominators, units, dates, causal claims, and comparisons.
- Are quotes exact and attributed, and is external work synthesized without copying its expression or distinctive structure?
- Are hypothetical examples marked, benchmarks contextualized, and untested code identified?
- Did the draft preserve the author's meaning, uncertainty, material experience, and requested inclusions?
- Do headings, paragraphs, lists, and the ending aid comprehension rather than impose a formula?
- Do images have a purpose, rights/credit information, and alt text matching the actual asset?
- Are private details, customer data, credentials, and identifiable third-party stories handled appropriately?
- Are material unresolved facts or publication requirements visible rather than hidden behind a “ready” claim?

Original value can come from research, explanation, synthesis, analysis, or experience. An article need not be impossible for another person to write. No word-count, sentence-count, hashtag, or CTR threshold proves editorial quality.

For current Medium policy and distribution questions, read [Medium mechanics](medium-mechanics.md). Keep platform eligibility separate from writing quality.
