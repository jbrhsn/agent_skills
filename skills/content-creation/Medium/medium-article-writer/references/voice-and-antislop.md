# Voice and substantive revision

Readable, specific prose is the goal. Punctuation, polished grammar, balanced reasoning, or a particular word cannot reliably identify who wrote a text. Do not add errors or awkwardness to disguise AI assistance.

Use the source's register and the intended reader's knowledge. Explain unfamiliar terms where needed; do not assume every reader knows the author's stack. Keep useful opinions and qualifications together. A strong view can still need evidence and boundaries.

Replace filler with the missing substance: which mechanism, what conditions, whose measurement, what tradeoff, which example. Remove repetitive openings, redundant conclusions, empty intensifiers, and headings that promise more than the section delivers.

Vary rhythm naturally with the thought, not by quotas for fragments, em dashes, or paragraph lengths. Lists, summaries, and familiar words are legitimate when they help the reader. Do not ban an effective structure solely because models also use it.

In technical work, make version and environment assumptions visible, separate illustrative output from observed results, and explain what a reader should verify. In narrative work, preserve truthful sequence, perspective, and emotional meaning without inventing dialogue or motives.

Read aloud or review paragraph transitions. The final piece should sound consistent, make its reasoning inspectable, and honor the author's meaning. Consult [voice inference](voice-inference.md) when fidelity is uncertain.
