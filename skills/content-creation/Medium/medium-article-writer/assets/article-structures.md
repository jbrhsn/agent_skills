# Article structure options

Adapt these shapes to the material and reader. They are prompts for reasoning, not required section names, lengths, or counts.

| Type | Useful progression | Evidence to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Tutorial | Task and prerequisites → approach → steps/example → verification → limits | Versions, executable assumptions, actual failures when supplied |
| Case study / post-mortem | Stakes → decisions → symptoms → diagnosis → repair → results and limits | Timeline, measurements, uncertainty, tradeoffs |
| Argument | Claim → reasons/evidence → strongest relevant objection → implications | Author's position and evidence boundaries |
| Explainer | Reader question → short answer → mechanism → example → application | Clear definitions, sources, limits of analogy |
| Experience report | Context → decision → outcome → reflection | Actual events, perspective, details and attribution |
| Research synthesis | Question → source selection → agreements/disagreements → interpretation → limitations | Source quality, dates, distinctions between evidence and inference |

An anecdote may establish stakes, explain a choice, or support a claim. Reposition or compress it when that improves the article without changing meaning. Significant omissions belong in notes; ask when they would alter the intended position or remove required material.

For nontechnical pieces, use the field's appropriate evidence rather than imposing engineering vocabulary. General educational finance/health/legal writing need not be memoir; verify consequential claims and preserve context, jurisdiction, dates, and uncertainty. Do not invent a license, personal result, testimonial, or product experience.
