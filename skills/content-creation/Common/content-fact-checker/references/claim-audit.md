# Claim audit reference

Use this reference while auditing a draft. It is a decision aid, not a required output format.

## Claim categories

| Category | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Personal experience | Preserve in the author's voice. Do not generalize it without evidence. |
| Opinion or recommendation | Preserve the judgment and tone; separately check factual premises. Flag ambiguous conflicts rather than choosing the author's position. |
| Stable background fact | Check its accuracy and scope; prioritize research depth according to its importance to the argument. |
| Current fact | Verify with a recent source and note the relevant date. |
| Statistic | Verify the exact number, population, geography, timeframe, and definition. |
| Forecast | Attribute it to the source and preserve uncertainty. Do not present a forecast as an outcome. |
| Causal claim | Look for evidence that supports the relationship, not merely evidence that both events occurred. |
| Universal or absolute claim | Usually qualify unless there is unusually strong evidence. |
| Vague quantity or comparison | Identify the measure, population, period, and baseline; narrow only to what relevant evidence supports. |

Track each claim's original wording, status, supporting or conflicting evidence, limitations, and proposed revision. “Unsupported” means the research did not establish the assertion; “contradicted” requires evidence against it; “unverified” means the necessary checking could not be completed. Do not collapse these into a verdict of falsehood.

## Evidence quality checks

Before attaching `[n]` to a sentence, confirm:

- The source directly supports the sentence, not merely its general topic.
- The source is appropriate for the claim. A vendor survey can show reported adoption among its sample, but cannot establish universal adoption.
- The cited number has not been broadened beyond the source's sample or timeframe.
- The source's publication date is suitable for the claim's time sensitivity.
- The wording distinguishes observed data, reported perceptions, estimates, and the author's interpretation.
- Relevant conflicting findings have been considered, with differences in definitions, populations, or periods explained rather than selectively omitted.
- Any calculated result uses compatible units and denominators, with the inputs cited and the calculation identified as derived rather than source-reported.

## Safe revision patterns

- Unsupported universal: replace “Everyone is using AI” with a verified survey result naming the sampled population, measure, and period, only if that narrower result still supports the author's point. Do not infer a trend from a single observation.
- Unsupported industry conclusion: “Companies no longer hire for coding” needs hiring evidence matching the stated industry and period. Narrow to the roles or organizations actually studied when supported; otherwise flag the gap. Adding “some” does not make an unresearched claim valid.
- Overconfident forecast: attribute the projection to its source with its horizon and assumptions. Merely changing “will” to “could” does not supply evidence for the prediction.
- Personal experience generalized: “This workflow is faster for me” remains personal unless a study supports a broader claim.
- Conflicting claims: state a distinction only when the draft and evidence support it. If resolving the conflict changes a central premise or the author's position, provide the proposed correction for review instead of silently rewriting the argument.

These are decision patterns, not researched facts or ready-to-paste claims. Never insert invented numbers or unresolved placeholders into a finished draft. Preserve personal judgments even when the evidence is mixed; qualify their empirical premises without rewriting the author's opinion.

## Reference-list checks

Use one stable number per source. Keep inline references close to the supported claim. Order the final list by first appearance unless the user requests another style. Include title, publisher or author, date when material, what the source supports, and a direct URL. Remove unused references and investigate any claim that has no matching source.
