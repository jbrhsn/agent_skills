# Structure and formatting

Choose a shape that fits the material: result and explanation, story and lesson, claim with evidence/counterargument, useful checklist, or direct announcement. These are options, not required zones or line counts.

Keep paragraphs readable and use blank lines where ideas change. Lists help when content is genuinely parallel or sequential. Avoid turning every sentence into a dramatic one-line paragraph. Length follows the requested format and substance; verify current composer limits if close to the boundary.

Use plain paste-ready text for a text post. Keep commentary, title-option labels, metadata, and character counts outside the post body. Prefer ordinary characters over decorative Unicode alphabet substitutions for readability, copying, and accessibility. Emojis and hashtags are optional and should match the author's tone and topic.

Citations and links may appear where they help readers inspect evidence. If a link belongs in notes or a comment for presentation reasons, retain enough attribution to avoid misleading readers.

Revise generic openings, repeated claims, vague adjectives, exaggerated contrasts, and forced motivational endings. Do not use word or punctuation blacklists as proof of AI authorship. Preserve useful terminology and natural rhythm.

End when the post has delivered its point. A takeaway, next action, announcement detail, or genuine question can work. Do not append a question merely to solicit comments.

Before delivery, check meaning against the source, attribution, support for numbers, accessibility, and the actual requested length. Count the final text if reporting a count; distinguish character count from byte count and note platform counting differences if relevant.
