# Calendar and review conventions

## Planning horizons

A weekly plan should be actionable for the specified dates. A monthly plan should cover the entire requested month, including partial weeks, with enough detail to begin production. Later timely slots may remain conditional with a refresh date and evergreen alternative. Do not claim four weeks cover every calendar month. Custom date ranges are supported.

Record the period and time zone once. Exact posting times are optional; without account evidence, label them experimental or leave them unset. Distinguish planned date, preparation deadline, and external scheduled status. Only observed account evidence establishes that something is scheduled or published.

## Compact calendar with linked briefs

Use a readable table and put long evidence and editorial details in item briefs. Stable identifiers such as `C01` connect calendar rows, research, adaptations, and later revisions. An X thread is one publishing unit with several component posts; state the counting convention when reporting cadence.

| Calendar field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ID and planned date | Stable item reference and publication target |
| Platform and format | Actual destination and suitable treatment |
| Topic and pillar | Working title and strategic purpose |
| Preparation and effort | Deadline, prerequisite, owner if known, estimated workload |
| Readiness | Proposed, needs evidence, needs author input, ready to draft, or observed production status |

Each linked brief supplies audience, intended outcome, reader problem, angle, evidence links/status, required original contribution, optional CTA/media, and adaptation relationships. Fields may be omitted when irrelevant. Do not imply that every piece requires promotion, a CTA, media, or numerical scoring.

## Feasibility

Sum estimated research, writing, adaptation, media, and review effort across destinations. Label estimates and avoid double-counting shared research while still counting each adaptation. Match the aggregate to the user's stated capacity and identify overloaded weeks. If capacity and requested cadence conflict, explain the conflict and offer a feasible alternative alongside the requested scope.

Put research, author input, asset preparation, and review before publication. When a piece depends on a published URL, the source must be available first or the derivative must have an explicit standalone fallback. A derivative based on shared research need not wait for the anchor article if it makes no link or publication promise. Publication acceptance or external review dates are uncertain unless confirmed.

For revisions, retain identifiers and completed work. Record changed dates, replaced angles, and reasons briefly. Recheck the entire affected dependency chain and workload; do not rewrite unrelated weeks or reclassify drafts as published.

## Learning from results

Choose measures based on the objective: relevant reader responses, article reading, qualified visits, or another observable outcome. Capture baseline availability, observation window, and the next review date. Define what would warrant repeating or changing the idea or format, without invented statistical certainty. Keep paid and organic results separate when known, and do not aggregate unlike platform metrics into a supposedly comparable reach score.
