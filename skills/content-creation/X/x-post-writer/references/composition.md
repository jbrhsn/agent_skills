# Compose for the reading experience

## Choose depth and form

Short, medium-length, and long describe how much development the idea needs. Respect a requested target, but do not manufacture universal length bands. A single-post request stays a single post unless the user agrees to a different form. When format is open, weigh continuity against useful segmentation, the reader's background, the available evidence, and known account limits.

| Form | Useful when | Editorial focus |
|---|---|---|
| Short single post | One observation, announcement, distinction, or question is sufficient | Make the point concrete; remove setup the reader does not need |
| Medium single post | A claim needs a reason, example, or caveat | Develop one central point without turning it into an unrelated list |
| Long single post | Context and qualifications need continuous reading | Establish the thesis early, group related material, and make each paragraph advance it |
| Thread | Steps, comparisons, evidence, or story beats deserve separate units | Give each post a role and preserve the connection between them |

The requested subject may be technical, personal, creative, educational, or commercial. Do not assume an engineering audience or force a lesson into a personal announcement.

## Single posts

Choose an opening that fits the material: a direct result, concrete observation, useful distinction, question, or scene. Curiosity should come from the substance; do not withhold the entire point, exaggerate stakes, or imply secrets the source cannot support.

For short posts, preserve the detail that makes the observation distinctive. Compression should remove repetition and throat-clearing before removing attribution or qualifications. A concise question can be the whole post when that is the user's purpose.

For medium posts, develop the point through a reason, example, comparison, or consequence. For long posts, arrange those elements into a continuous argument or narrative; a small outline is useful when the dependencies are complicated. Use paragraphs and occasional descriptive signposts where they improve reading. A longer allowance is a ceiling, not a writing target.

End when the piece has fulfilled its purpose. An actionable next step, implication, open question, or invitation can fit, but none is mandatory. Keep promotional requests proportionate to the actual offer and user brief.

## Threads

Map the sequence before polishing individual posts when the material needs it. Possible shapes include problem → mechanism → example → tradeoff; observation → investigation → result → limitation; or a set of linked comparisons. These are options, not templates that every thread must follow.

The opener should deliver meaningful information and establish why the rest is worth reading. Each subsequent post advances the idea rather than repeating the opener. Split at thought boundaries, not arbitrary character offsets. Add concise transitions where the relationship would otherwise be unclear.

Write for readers who may encounter a middle post first. Give it enough local context to avoid misleading interpretations without restating the entire thread. Keep a claim's crucial caveat in the same post; a qualification several posts later may never reach the same reader. Place source credit near the claim and use a final source post only when earlier posts remain adequately attributed.

Choose thread length from the substance and requested constraints. Avoid padding, a fixed post quota, repeated cliffhangers, or an obligatory follow/repost ending. In-copy numbering is optional; if used, finalize the total and count its characters after editing. Review the whole sequence after any split or deletion.

## Adaptation and revision

Treat a source article as material to reorganize, not paragraphs to mechanically truncate. Preserve its argument, strongest evidence, and consequential limits. If the requested short form cannot contain an explicitly required point, raise that tradeoff rather than quietly deleting it. For a narrow edit, keep the user's chosen structure unless it causes the problem being fixed.

Keep the author's natural diction, humor, uncertainty, and register. Avoid replacing distinctive writing with repetitive rhetorical contrasts, generic authority claims, or motivational filler. Style is not evidence of authorship, and editorial polish should not be presented as a way to conceal AI use.

Use plain text by default. Blank lines can separate thoughts; not every sentence needs its own paragraph. Avoid decorative Unicode alphabets used to simulate bold. Emojis and hashtags are optional, voice-dependent choices; they consume space and should have a clear purpose. A useful link belongs where readers need it, without an assumed universal distribution penalty.

If media would explain something better, suggest the specific asset and its role. Exact charts, screenshots, and diagrams need real data or identified examples. Alt text should describe the actual inspected asset; a proposed visual can only have provisional alt text.
